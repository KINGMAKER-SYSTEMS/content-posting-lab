OpenAI's own team shipped a million-line codebase with zero lines of manually written code. They call the discipline harness engineering. Three engineers, growing to seven over five months, drove Codex to that million lines across roughly fifteen hundred merged pull requests — three and a half per engineer per day, an estimated one tenth the hand-written time. The trail: GPT-5-Codex, September fifteenth, 2025 — working independently for more than seven hours, fixing its own test failures. Then GPT-5.1-Codex-Max shipped compaction — native training across multiple context windows — and OpenAI reported internal tasks past twenty-four hours. Reality check: METR measured its fifty percent time horizon around two hours forty minutes, versus GPT-5's two hours seventeen. The bridge is the harness. February twenty-third, 2026, OpenAI's dev blog: Codex ran for about twenty-five hours uninterrupted, used about thirteen million tokens, and generated about thirty thousand lines of code — four durable markdown memory files, verification commands after milestones, credit to the loop, not one-shot intelligence. OpenAI named the layer: the Codex harness, the agent loop under every Codex experience — threads you resume, fork, archive. The long-horizon coding agent got productized. The product is the loop.
